Core Research Pillars

Bridging non-equilibrium statistical physics, active matter rheology, living matter and biological transport, and exact analytical dynamics.

01

Emergent Rheology & Active Unjamming

Investigating non-equilibrium flow, yielding, and deformation in dense disordered active mixtures and amorphous glasses under steady shear. We discovered that a dilute fraction of active dopants fluidizes a dense amorphous packing as effectively as uniform activity, with steady shear flow curves, yield stress, and fluidization scaling with active energy. This work provides a quantitative framework for how minimal active constituents induce non-affine rearrangements to mechanically remodel dense suspensions, tissues, and active composites.

Featured in: Phys. Rev. Lett. 135, 168302 (2025)
02

Dense Active Solids & Flocking Dynamics

Developing normal-mode elasticity and continuum theories for collective order and arrest in crowded self-propelled systems. We proved that intrinsic chirality and rotational noise in active solids drive soft modes, generating macroscopic velocity correlations extending far beyond direct contact. In dense polar active disks, we demonstrated spontaneous noise-induced quenched disorder where angular fluctuations arrest collective motion, and coauthored the landmark review synthesizing self-aligning polar active matter.

Featured in: Rev. Mod. Phys. 97, 015007 (2025) PRL 131, 168301 (2023)
03

Living Matter & Biological Transport

Formulating quantitative stochastic and mechanical models for intracellular transport and molecular motors in close dialogue with experimental data. In C. elegans neurons, we showed that kinesin-3 (UNC-104) motor trafficking and steady-state distributions remain remarkably robust to large changes in cargo-binding kinetics, while post-translational modifications regulate anterograde transport. In filament–motor systems, we uncovered load-induced limit-cycle oscillations and activity-driven re-entrant open-to-spiral transitions in gliding assays.

Featured in: Phys. Rev. E (Editors' Suggestion 2025) J. Cell Sci. 137, jcs.261553 (2024)
04

Exact Stochastic Dynamics & Resetting Protocols

Developing solvable non-equilibrium statistical mechanics formulations and renewal theories for self-propelled agents. We established a foundational mathematical mapping between active Brownian trajectories and semi-flexible polymers, calculating closed-form expressions for displacement moments, excess kurtosis, and bistabilities under torque, confinement, and speed fluctuations. In sole-author work, we formulated exact renewal descriptions of active Brownian motion subject to stochastic position and orientation resetting, revealing how intermittent restarts steer chiral motion and optimize first-passage search.

Featured in: Phys. Rev. E 113, 025424 (2026) Soft Matter 16, 4776 (2020)
